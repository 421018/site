## The problem {#problem}

When many people or devices record the same thing (a measurement, an event, a delivery), the set usually lives in one organization's system. The others have to trust that it will not be altered and that it will stay available.

## How the protocol solves it {#solution}

Each contribution is recorded on the chain. All the copies match. If someone changes a datum that is already closed, it shows. Anyone can audit the set without asking the person who administers it for permission.

## An example {#example}

A network of neighbors measures the air quality in their neighborhood.

- Each sensor or person records their reading.
- The set stays on the shared ledger.
- Months later, anyone can verify what was measured and whether something changed.

The same pattern works for community events, aid deliveries or any fact that several parties need to remember together.

## What goes on the chain and what does not {#data}

| On the chain | Off the chain |
|---|---|
| The readings or events and their fingerprints | Sensitive or heavy data |
| Who contributed each one | Full identities when they are not needed |

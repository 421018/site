## The problem {#problem}

When a file, a model or a data set passes through several people, its history is usually lost or stays on one organization's server. Afterwards it is hard to show who modified it and when.

## How the protocol solves it {#solution}

Each relevant step is recorded with the fingerprint of the content and the signature of whoever touched it. The chain of provenance stays in view. Anyone who has the file can check its history.

## An example {#example}

A data model passes through creation, review and publication.

- Whoever creates it records the first fingerprint.
- Whoever reviews it records their version.
- Whoever publishes it records the final version.
- Later it can be shown exactly which version was used and who took part.

The same pattern works for documents, data sets or any digital work that needs a clear history.

## What goes on the chain and what does not {#data}

| On the chain | Off the chain |
|---|---|
| The fingerprints and signatures of each step | The full file |
| The dates | Sensitive content |

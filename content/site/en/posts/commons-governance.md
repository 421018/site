## The problem {#problem}

When a group administers something shared (a fund, a space, a rule), the decisions usually stay in the system of whoever organizes it. The others have to trust that the result will not be altered afterwards.

## How the protocol solves it {#solution}

Proposals and votes are recorded on the chain. The result stays fixed and can be checked. Nobody can change what was already decided without it showing.

## An example {#example}

A cooperative decides how to use a common fund.

- Someone submits a proposal.
- The members vote.
- The result stays on the record.
- Months later anyone can verify what was approved and who voted.

The same pattern works for the rules of a space, a community's priorities or a collective's decisions.

## What goes on the chain and what does not {#data}

| On the chain | Off the chain |
|---|---|
| The proposal and the votes | Private debates or drafts |
| The result and the date | Personal data that is not needed |

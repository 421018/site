## The problem {#problem}

Access to a space, a piece of data or a service usually depends on a central gatekeeper who keeps the rules and decides. If that gatekeeper changes the rules or stops being there, access gets complicated.

## How the protocol solves it {#solution}

The access conditions are recorded on the chain. Anyone can verify whether they are met without asking an intermediary. The rules stay in view and cannot be changed in silence.

## An example {#example}

A community defines who can use a shared space or resource.

- The conditions are written down.
- Whoever wants access presents the proof.
- Verification is done directly against the ledger.

The same pattern works for data, services or any access that several parties need to govern together.

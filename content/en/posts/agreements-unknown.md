## The problem {#problem}

When two people who do not know each other want to exchange something or keep a commitment, they usually need an intermediary. That intermediary holds the value, keeps the record and, if there is a dispute, decides. Without them, each party has to trust the other.

## How the protocol solves it {#solution}

The protocol lets the two parties leave a record of the agreement and of its fulfillment on a shared ledger. No platform has to hold the value or interpret the rules. Whoever wants to check what happened does so directly.

## An example {#example}

Ana offers a service. Bruno needs it. They do not know each other.

- They agree on the terms and record them.
- Bruno confirms the payment or the reservation.
- Ana confirms that she fulfilled it.
- Either of them (or a third party) can verify the state without asking a platform.

The same pattern works for reservations, exchanges or one-off collaborations.

## What goes on the chain and what does not {#data}

| On the chain | Off the chain |
|---|---|
| The agreement and its confirmations | The private details of the service |
| The accounts that took part | Personal data |

A basis for markets, services and collaborations without a central intermediary.

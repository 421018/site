## Nature of this site {#independence}

This site is informational: it presents the project, explains how the protocol works and how to use it, and shows the state of the network.

This site does not offer, sell or recommend the native unit, Improbability (42), or any other digital asset, nor platforms or intermediaries. Its mentions of the native unit are exclusively technical: they explain how the protocol works. Nothing on it is financial, legal, tax or investment advice.

## Who does what {#roles}

The contributors are the people who develop, review and publish the project's source code and content: the software of the protocol and its tools, and this site. That is their role, and they do it in the open, so that anyone can review the work, propose changes or contribute.

What happens on the chain is not in their hands:

- **Governance of the network.** Each network that runs the protocol is operated by its validators, and its rules change only through on-chain governance: proposals that the validators and those who delegate units to them vote on. A new version of the software reaches a network only if that network approves it and its validators adopt it. The contributors can propose changes like anyone else, but they do not decide them. [Governance](docs:/protocol/governance)
- **Applications and services.** Contracts, applications, wallets, RPC providers, explorers and other services built on or around the network belong to those who build and run them, who are responsible for them. None of them is official. [Third-party services](#third-parties)
- **Operations on the chain.** Every transaction is signed and sent by its user, who decides it and is responsible for it. The contributors do not hold anyone's assets or keys, and cannot reverse transactions or restore access to an account.

## Risks of use {#risks}

Using the network has risks worth understanding before taking part:

- **Loss of access.** If an account's recovery phrase or private keys are lost, there is no way to recover access.
- **Final transactions.** A confirmed transaction cannot be reversed.
- **Third-party contracts.** Contracts published by third parties can contain errors or be made to deceive. The protocol runs them exactly as written.
- **Errors in the software.** The protocol software is reviewed, but it can contain errors that have not been found yet.
- **Upgrades.** Upgrades approved by governance may require those who run nodes or integrated services to update their software in time.
- **Regulation.** Each person and organization is responsible for complying with the laws that apply to them when using the network and its tools.

## Privacy {#privacy}

This site is built so that as little data as possible passes through it:

- **No accounts or forms.** It does not ask for personal data.
- **No cookies or analytics.** It does not use cookies or analytics or advertising tools. It only keeps a few preferences in your browser, which never leave it: the theme (light or dark), the selected network and whether you closed a notice.
- **Local search.** The search index is downloaded from the site itself, and what you search for is not sent to any server.
- **Network queries.** The tools that show the network live read public data, such as the latest block or the gas price, straight from your browser from the public services (RPC) of the independent providers in the [directory](docs:/get-started/connect#providers). Those providers receive your IP address, as with any connection, under their own policies. Nothing else is sent.
- **No other third-party resources.** Fonts and other resources are served from the site itself. Apart from those queries, the browser connects to another site only when you follow an external link or ask your wallet to add a network.
- **Hosting.** Like any website, the hosting provider (Cloudflare) processes technical data of each visit, such as the IP address, in order to deliver the pages and protect them from abuse, under its own privacy policy.

Everything recorded on the network, such as addresses, transactions or contracts, is public and permanent, and cannot be erased. That is why personal data should not be recorded on the chain. [What goes on and off the chain](docs:/build/applications#application)

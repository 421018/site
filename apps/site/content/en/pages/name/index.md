## An exact answer {#answer}

In *The Hitchhiker’s Guide to the Galaxy*, Douglas Adams's novel, a supercomputer spends millions of years working out the answer to the great question of life, the universe and everything. The answer is 42. Exact, impeccable, and nobody quite remembers what the question was.

It seemed a fair description of infrastructure: it gives precise, checkable answers, and leaves the meaning to those who use it.

## An improbable engine {#engine}

In the same novel, ships cross the galaxy with an *Infinite Improbability Drive*, an engine that for an instant makes possible what seemed impossible. That is where our engine's name comes from, Infinite Drive: a name of its own, with a nod to where it came from.

## A cup of tea {#tea}

In the story, the drive starts with a properly made cup of tea. We take that seriously, more or less: the unit the network runs on is called Improbability and its symbol is 42. The smallest fraction is a **drop**, and a whole unit is colloquially a **cup**.

So when someone says they will do something "on 42", they almost always mean the network. And when they say they are short of a cup, it is worth asking what they mean.
